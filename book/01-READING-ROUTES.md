## Study routine
Recurring teaching blocks support the six-step study routine below, but layouts and source coverage vary by unit. This six-step routine is distinct from the seven-element teaching template described in the usage guide. Re-derive assumptions and units, and allow additional time for equations, code, exercises and empirical checks.


| Step | Existing section | Your task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | TL;DR and The story | You state the problem and explain the central idea in plain language. |
| 2 | Decoder table and Core mechanics | You define the terms and work through the mechanism, trade-offs, and failure cases. |
| 3 | Diagrams and Key numbers | You redraw the system and reproduce the important calculations with units. |
| 4 | Whiteboard pack | You draw the system from memory and give the spoken explanation. |
| 5 | Interview traps | You answer the questions before reading the supplied answers. |
| 6 | Question Bank and Design Checklists | Use self-tests and checklists to find omissions and untested assumptions, not to certify that every risk is covered. |


Core, Senior and Staff are the source’s practice tags for mechanisms, constrained derivation and judgment under changed conditions; they are not independently calibrated hiring levels. Practice with [A2\_question\_bank](appendices/A2_question_bank.md) and review with [A3\_design\_checklists](appendices/A3_design_checklists.md).
A useful study checkpoint is an explanation with a choice, definitions, units, cost, failure mode and reversal condition. Unaided recall is one practice tool, not proof of correctness; verify with references, calculations and tests when making engineering decisions.
